TOKYO, JAPAN

01 VISION ／ What we are aiming for

Turning a district itself
into one experience.

Not touring the sights, but stepping inside the story of a place. That is the kind of travel we want to build.

In Asakusa, for example:the Edo story begins the moment you enter the hotel.Step outside and there are long-established shops, workshops, museums, restaurants, shrines and back alleys — each holding an experience that exists only there.

Not “I toured Asakusa,”
but “I stepped into the story of Asakusa.”

We are not trying to build a theme park inside a hotel. What we ultimately want isto turn a district itself into a single experience.

But a whole district cannot be moved at once.So we are starting with hotels.

02 FIRST HYPOTHESIS ／ Our first business hypothesis

Starting with hotels.

Our first hypothesis

Raising the value of existing hotels
for inbound guests.

Guests from overseas are already arriving. But we suspect there are properties not yet capturing the full value of that. That is our starting thought.

Issues we suspect may exist

  • Overseas guests are coming, but it may be hard to stand apart from other properties
  • Room rates and ancillary revenue might be able to go a little higher
  • There may be a wish to renovate, but difficulty judging what investment would actually pay back

These are still hypotheses. We are in the middle of asking people on the ground whether they hold true. We are not in a position to assert them.

03 APPROACH ／ How we work

We start by
understanding the customer.

We do not arrive with the answer already decided.First we learn the operation and the numbers. Then we work out what to build that would raise value, implement it small, and look at the result.

What we are not

  • We are not a company that sells AR
  • We are not a company that sells AI tools
  • We are not a company that runs a survey and stops there

We will use any of them where useful. None of them is ever the point.

  1. 01UnderstandGo on site and learn how the operation and the numbers actually work.
  2. 02DesignForm a hypothesis about what would actually raise value.
  3. 03Build smallOne room, one spot. Built at a scale you can try without shutting anything down.
  4. 04MeasureADR, occupancy, ancillary revenue, satisfaction. Check what moved.
  5. 05ExpandIf the numbers come through, move to the next room, then the next property.
Concept visual — not an actual completed project

04 ONE ROOM

Testing it first
in a single room.

We call it the One Room Project, butit is not a finished product. It is a unit of testing.

There is no need to change the whole property. Use one room, build an experience that could only exist there, look at the numbers, and expand if it works. That is all.

  1. 01Try one room
  2. 02Measure
  3. 03Expand if it works

We assume it must be testable without losing occupancy, and fully reversible. We want to start from a position where stopping is still a choice.

05 WHO WE ARE ／ About the founder

Not a hotel expert,
but someone who makes
businesses work.

I do not come from the hotel industry.Please read what follows with that in mind.

My background is in B2B equipment deployment and new business development, working across labour utilisation, materials, cost, pricing, margin, man-hours, lead time, project P&L, partners and construction, andcarrying it all the way through to actual on-site deployment.

In a hotel, the question of how rooms, staffing, equipment, ADR, occupancy, ancillary revenue and CAPEX combine into a business that works is, I believe, the same question.

Find the business problem,
combine the people, technology and equipment,
and carry it through to something that works as a business.

Shota Yamamoto

What I have worked with ／ What it maps to in a hotel

  • Labour utilisation, man-hours→Staffing and operations
  • Materials, cost, price, margin→CAPEX, ADR, ancillary revenue
  • Lead time→Build time and room downtime
  • Project P&L→Return on investment
  • Partners and construction→Design, construction and operation

I have no operating experience in the accommodation industry. We do not present improvements in ADR or occupancy as proven results at this stage.

06 WHERE WE ARE NOW ／ Where we are now

Right now, we are
listening.

At present we are asking hotel operators, owners, asset managers, planning teams and general managers for their time, to hear what things are actually like on the ground.

This is not a sales call.Proposing anything without knowing what is actually happening would be meaningless.

What we ask

  • What renovations or equipment investments have you made recently?
  • On what basis do you decide whether to invest?
  • What are you actually struggling with right now?
  • What renovations are you considering going forward?

Thirty minutes is enough. We will not bring a deck, and we will not pitch on the spot.
